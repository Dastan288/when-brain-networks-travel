%% title
When Brain Networks Travel: Cross-Dataset Generalization of Connectome-Based Classifiers across ABIDE, ADHD-200 and UCLA-CNP

%% abstract
Connectome-based classifiers for neurodevelopmental and psychiatric disorders are usually validated inside one repository, often with cross-validation that mixes acquisition sites. We asked whether their predictive information transfers between repositories. With one connectome representation (90 cerebral AAL regions) and fixed hyperparameters, we evaluated every ordered training–test pair of ABIDE I (autism; 1033 participants, 17 sites), ADHD-200 (939 participants, 7 analyzed sites) and UCLA-CNP (261 participants, two scanners), plus a diagnosis-matched ADHD transfer stress test between ADHD-200 and the UCLA-CNP ADHD subset, which also differs in age and preprocessing. We used leave-one-site-out validation and an age-and-sex baseline. Within ABIDE, logistic regression reached a site-averaged balanced accuracy of 0.64 [0.60, 0.68]. Its pooled estimate barely changed when linear age and sex effects were removed from every edge. Within ADHD-200 it was at chance (0.50), whereas age and sex alone reached 0.60. No cross-repository cell, including the diagnosis-matched pair, showed a statistically supported advantage in balanced accuracy over the age-and-sex baseline. ComBat, evaluated without target statistics and with unlabeled target-batch adjustment, changed balanced accuracy by at most 0.053. Under the tested repository shifts, the evaluated pipelines showed little evidence of transferable case-control connectivity signal.

resting-state fMRI; functional connectivity; cross-dataset generalization; external validation; site effects; ComBat harmonization; leave-one-site-out; trustworthy clinical AI

# Introduction

Open repositories such as ABIDE [dimartino2014], ADHD-200 [adhd200consortium2012] and UCLA-CNP [poldrack2016] allow resting-state functional connectivity (rs-FC) classifiers to be trained on hundreds of participants, but most reported accuracies come from cross-validation that mixes scanning sites between training and test folds. Scanner, protocol and cohort differences leave a strong imprint on rs-FC, and site effects can be as large as disorder effects [yamashita2019]. A classifier can therefore use site as a proxy and appear to generalize when it does not, a form of leakage [kapoor2023] quantified for connectome models by Rosenblatt et al. [rosenblatt2024]. Leave-one-site-out evaluation is the standard response [nielsen2013, abraham2017]. External validation is also a precondition for trustworthy AI in digital healthcare [woo2017]: schizophrenia prediction models that performed well within one trial were at chance in other trials [chekroud2024].

The three repositories do not share one diagnostic task, so we separate two questions. **Question A (cross-repository, cross-disorder stress test):** does a case-control connectivity signature learned in one repository carry over to another repository with a different diagnostic task? A near-chance result here has two explanations: no transferable representation, or simply that autism- and ADHD-related patterns differ. **Question B (diagnosis-matched transfer stress test):** does an ADHD classifier transfer between ADHD-200 and the ADHD subset of UCLA-CNP? This comparison matches the diagnostic label but not the age distribution, acquisition, cohort composition or preprocessing, so it does not isolate repository shift alone.

ComBat [johnson2007, fortin2018] is the standard method for removing site effects, but in prediction studies it is often fitted on pooled training and test data, which leaks test-set statistics [marzi2024]. We contribute (i) leave-one-site-out baselines with subject- *and* site-level uncertainty, the optimism of site-mixed cross-validation, a demographics-only control and a demographically adjusted feature set; (ii) the complete ordered transfer matrix across the three primary repositories, supplemented by the diagnosis-matched UCLA-CNP ADHD analysis; and (iii) a leakage-aware evaluation of ComBat in two separated modes, with paired, multiplicity-corrected estimates.

# Related work

Multi-site classification has a limited record of external validity: ABIDE multi-site accuracy is about 60–67 % [nielsen2013, abraham2017], and in a blinded autism biomarker challenge the best team fell from about 0.80 to 0.72 AUC on an independent cohort [traut2022]. The ADHD-200 Global Competition was won by a phenotype-only submission [adhd200consortium2012]. Graph neural networks are now benchmarked on brain networks [cui2023], but their behavior under repository shift is unresolved, which is one reason we evaluate simple, auditable models.

ComBat, an empirical-Bayes location/scale model [johnson2007], has been adapted to neuroimaging [fortin2018]; nonlinear alternatives are reviewed in [hu2023]. Applying ComBat before the train/test split leaks information [marzi2024]; we follow this discipline and separate a ComBat-derived reference-only transformation of held-out data from the transductive new-batch mode.

# Materials and methods

## Datasets and classification tasks

We used only openly released, already-preprocessed derivatives (Table 1).

**ABIDE I** [dimartino2014] aggregates 1112 resting-state scans (539 autism spectrum disorder, ASD; 573 typical controls) from 17 sites. We used the Preprocessed Connectomes Project C-PAC release [craddock2013] with the *filt_noglobal* strategy (band-pass; white-matter, cerebrospinal-fluid and motion regression; no global-signal regression, GSR). It provides time series for 1035 participants; institutions listed under two identifiers were merged into one site. Rater-based quality flags define a sensitivity subset (n = 871). The *filt_global* release served as a robustness check and as the GSR-matched partner of ADHD-200.

**ADHD-200** [adhd200consortium2012] contains 973 children, adolescents and young adults with ADHD or typically developing controls (TDC) from 8 sites. We used the Athena-pipeline AAL time series [bellec2017] (white-matter, cerebrospinal-fluid, motion and polynomial regression; band-pass 0.009–0.08 Hz; 6 mm smoothing; 4 mm MNI space; no GSR documented) and took the filtered first resting run per participant. Time series were available for 965 participants. After excluding the 26 Brown participants, whose diagnostic labels were never released, the analyzed cohort comprised 939 participants (357 ADHD / 582 TDC) from 7 sites. Two analyzed sites have extreme class balance (WashU: controls only; Pittsburgh: 4 ADHD among 98); both were kept, but per-site metrics are undefined for the single-class site.

**UCLA-CNP** [poldrack2016] (ds000030) contains 130 healthy controls and patients with schizophrenia (n = 50), bipolar disorder (n = 49) and ADHD (n = 43), scanned at one site on two scanners. From the released fMRIPrep [esteban2019] derivatives [gorgolewski2017] we extracted time series for the 261 participants with a resting-state run. We regressed mean white matter, the first anatomical CompCor component (the released confounds contain no cerebrospinal-fluid regressor) and six motion parameters, with detrending and band-pass, again without GSR. The two scanners (n = 171 and 90; 41 % and 76 % patients) serve as its sites. The task is any diagnosis vs control; the ADHD subset (40 vs 122 controls) is the diagnosis-matched partner of ADHD-200.

A participant was excluded only when more than 20 % of regions had zero variance (two in ABIDE); correlations involving a zero-variance region were set to zero. Age and sex were available for all participants. Although GSR is not part of the documented Athena pipeline, the ADHD-200 connectomes resemble global-signal-regressed data: mean Fisher-z over all edges 0.03 with 56 % of edges negative, as in ABIDE *filt_global* (0.02, 58 %) and unlike ABIDE *filt_noglobal* (0.37, 0 %) or UCLA-CNP (0.32, 1 %). This does not prove that GSR was applied, so we kept the pre-specified design and added a GSR-matched control.

## Connectome representation and evaluation protocols

The study equalizes the *downstream representation*, not the preprocessing: all datasets were reduced to the same 90 cerebral AAL regions [tzourio2002]; cerebellar and vermal regions were dropped because their coverage varies across scanners and acts as a site marker. Pearson correlations were Fisher-z transformed and the upper triangle was vectorized (4005 edges), without feature selection. AAL-116 and a functional 200-region parcellation [craddock2012] served as robustness checks within ABIDE.

Leave-one-site-out cross-validation held out one site at a time; for UCLA-CNP this is leave-one-scanner-out. All applicable preprocessing operations were fitted on training data only: demographic residualization (in the *conn-resid* analysis only), standardization, harmonization where applicable, and the classifier. We pre-specified the pooled out-of-fold balanced accuracy as the headline estimate and also report the macro-average of per-site values. When sites differ strongly in class prior, the pooled estimate combines predictions from models fitted under different site and class compositions and can fall below chance for that reason alone. We therefore treat the site-macro average as the primary site-aware summary for every repository and the pooled estimate as secondary; both are always reported. Stratified 10-fold cross-validation pooled across sites, averaged over 20 shuffles, is the optimistic contrast. For cross-dataset transfer, a model fitted on 100 % of a source dataset was applied unchanged to 100 % of a target dataset, for every ordered pair.

## Classifiers, demographic control and demographic adjustment

We used L2-regularized logistic regression, a linear-kernel SVM (both C = 1 and class-weighted) and a random forest (500 trees). Hyperparameters were fixed a priori, and features were standardized with training-set statistics only. A logistic regression on age and sex alone served as the demographic control (scikit-learn [pedregosa2011] and nilearn [abraham2014]; seed 42; all package versions pinned in the analysis environment). Because connectivity itself encodes age and sex, we added two stricter comparisons, both fitted on training data only. *conn+age+sex* appends age and sex to the connectome and tests whether they add predictive value. *conn-resid* residualizes every edge on age and sex (edge = b₀ + b₁·age + b₂·sex + e).

## Harmonization and statistics

ComBat [johnson2007, fortin2018] used site as batch and age and sex as preserved covariates. Its reference model (per-site location and scale, a covariate model and a pooled reference) was always fitted on the training sites. *Reference-only* (a ComBat-derived reference-only transformation) maps held-out participants with the training covariate model and pooled reference and uses no held-out statistics. *New-batch* additionally treats the held-out set as its own batch, with location and scale estimated from its unlabeled features; this is transductive and corresponds to cohort-level deployment. Our implementation reproduces neuroCombat [fortin2018] to within 10⁻⁷. ComBat is undefined for a single-site training set and was never fitted on pooled training and test data.

Balanced accuracy (bAcc) and ROC-AUC are reported with 95 % confidence intervals (CI) from 1000 subject-level bootstrap resamples of the held-out predictions. Because subject-level intervals are conditional on the sites evaluated, the site-macro CI resamples sites (2000 draws; single-class sites do not contribute), and a cluster bootstrap over sites is added for the pooled estimate. Differences between feature sets, models and harmonization modes are paired bootstrap differences on identical held-out participants. Two-sided bootstrap p-values were Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) corrected within each experiment and metric: 60 harmonization comparisons (3 classifiers × 10 evaluable cells × 2 ComBat modes), 42 connectome-versus-control comparisons and 40 demographic-adjustment comparisons; single unadjusted intervals are exploratory. Model interpretation used mean logistic-regression weights across folds, with stability measured by the Spearman correlation of absolute weights and top-100 Jaccard overlap. Cross-dataset top-100 overlaps were tested against a permutation null (2000 draws of 100 edges).

# Results

## Within-repository baselines

Table 1 gives the leave-one-site-out results for the primary model. In ABIDE, logistic regression on 4005 AAL-90 edges reached a site-macro bAcc of 0.642 [0.605, 0.676], with per-site values from 0.50 to 0.76. The pooled estimate was 0.649 [0.620, 0.677] (site-cluster CI [0.618, 0.678]); the SVM (0.634) and random forest (0.649) were similar. Site-mixed 10-fold cross-validation gave 0.652 ± 0.009 over 20 repeats, so it showed no measurable optimism in ABIDE. Robustness checks agreed: rater-QC subset 0.631, AAL-116 0.635, the GSR release *filt_global* 0.653 [0.625, 0.680] and a functional 200-region parcellation 0.682 [0.653, 0.709], as in [abraham2017].

ADHD-200 behaved differently. Within ADHD-200, site-macro bAcc was 0.503 [0.486, 0.516], indicating chance-level site-independent performance; per-site values ranged from 0.47 to 0.52 and per-site AUC never exceeded 0.55. The pooled out-of-fold estimate was lower (0.459 [0.430, 0.492]) because it aggregates predictions from models fitted under markedly different site and class compositions. The SVM and random forest were also at chance by site-macro (0.508 and 0.485), with pooled estimates slightly below 0.5. Age and sex alone reached a site-macro bAcc of 0.602 [0.558, 0.632] (pooled 0.609 [0.579, 0.639]). Site-mixed cross-validation gave 0.575 ± 0.008 (mean ± SD over 20 repeats), 0.07 above the site-macro and 0.12 above the pooled leave-one-site-out estimate. Taken alone, this number would have suggested a working classifier, although age and sex reached 0.626 ± 0.004 under the same protocol.

In UCLA-CNP, leave-one-scanner-out gave a site-macro bAcc of 0.589 and a pooled 0.525 [0.469, 0.582], against 0.567 and 0.558 [0.499, 0.620] for age and sex, and 0.606 ± 0.016 under site-mixed cross-validation; with two scanners that differ in class prior, this diagonal is a stress test only. The UCLA-CNP ADHD subset was at or below chance (0.416 [0.334, 0.497]).


## Cross-repository transfer

Fig. 1 shows the transfer matrix for logistic regression without harmonization (a) and with reference-only (b) and new-batch ComBat (c); the SVM and random-forest matrices (repository) agree. No off-diagonal cell showed a statistically supported advantage in bAcc over the age-and-sex baseline. The largest entry, ABIDE→UCLA-CNP at 0.589 [0.532, 0.646], was above chance but did not differ from the age-and-sex model (0.547 [0.490, 0.606]; paired difference 0.042 [−0.034, 0.120]). For ABIDE→ADHD-200 (0.529 [0.500, 0.554]) the demographic control was clearly better (0.631 [0.603, 0.661]), consistent with the male excess among patients in both repositories. Models trained on ADHD-200 were at or below chance on the other repositories (0.514 on ABIDE, 0.474 on UCLA-CNP).

The diagnosis-matched comparison was also near chance: ADHD-200→UCLA-ADHD 0.529 [0.443, 0.621] and UCLA-ADHD→ADHD-200 0.523 [0.497, 0.548]. Here the age-and-sex control is degenerate in bAcc (0.500 with a zero-width interval: one class predicted for the whole target set, with training and test ages barely overlapping), so the relevant reference is chance.

## Harmonization and demographic adjustment

Table 2 combines the two adjustments. ComBat changed no conclusion in either mode. Across the 10 logistic-regression cells where it is defined, bAcc differences ranged from −0.053 to +0.038. Six of 60 comparisons over all models had intervals excluding zero, and one survived BH correction: new-batch adjustment *within* ADHD-200, +0.038 [0.015, 0.061], which moved the pooled estimate from below chance to chance (0.459 → 0.497). For logistic regression, reference-only ComBat was indistinguishable from no harmonization in every cell (|Δ| ≤ 0.028). In the GSR-matched control, ABIDE *filt_global*→ADHD-200 reached 0.537 [0.510, 0.565], still below its demographic control (0.631), and ADHD-200→ABIDE *filt_global* 0.539 [0.509, 0.569], not different from its control (0.531). Matching GSR thus changed transfer by at most 0.025.

Within ABIDE, residualizing every edge left performance almost unchanged (0.644 [0.614, 0.672]; paired difference −0.005 [−0.020, 0.010]), still 0.122 [0.083, 0.157] above the control (p~FDR~ < 0.001). Appending age and sex added nothing material (+0.004 [−0.004, 0.011]). The ABIDE signal is therefore not explained by the modeled linear age- and sex-related effects. ABIDE→UCLA-CNP, in contrast, fell from 0.589 to 0.545 [0.484, 0.606], a paired loss of −0.043 [−0.077, −0.010] (unadjusted p = 0.014, p~FDR~ = 0.051), and after adjustment it was indistinguishable from age and sex alone (−0.001 [−0.086, 0.083]). The diagnosis-matched cells fell to 0.498 and 0.502; the UCLA-ADHD→ADHD-200 drop, −0.021 [−0.035, −0.008] (p~FDR~ = 0.008), is the one bAcc attenuation that survives correction. This attenuation is consistent with demographic confounding contributing to the apparent cross-repository transfer.

The learned weights agree: stable across ABIDE folds (mean Spearman ρ = 0.75; top-100 Jaccard 0.45), unstable in ADHD-200 (ρ = 0.49), and unrelated between the two repositories (ρ = 0.02; top-100 Jaccard 0.010, permutation p = 0.71).

# Discussion

## Interpretation

Within ABIDE, a plain linear classifier is comparatively stable across sites, and its advantage over the age-and-sex control persists after the modeled linear age- and sex-related component is removed from every edge. Across repositories (Question A), the one positive-looking cell, ABIDE→UCLA-CNP, moves toward the demographic baseline after residualization. ComBat moved bAcc by a few hundredths; the one effect that survived correction removed a site offset within ADHD-200 but did not recover a disorder signal.

The pipeline finds no site-independent ADHD signal even within ADHD-200, consistent with the phenotype-only winner of the original competition, and site-mixed cross-validation would have hidden this. We call the gap optimism rather than leakage in the narrow sense, because random and leave-one-site-out splits also differ in training composition; its size (absent in ABIDE, 0.07–0.12 in ADHD-200) must be measured per repository. The demographic control is equally necessary: age and sex predicted ADHD status at 0.60–0.63, so a classifier can appear to transfer for reasons unrelated to connectivity [woo2017].

The diagnosis-matched ADHD comparison also remained near chance. It should not be read as a clean repository-only transfer test. It involves a major developmental shift (cohort mean age 12.0 versus 31.7 years), and resting-state connectivity changes substantially between childhood and adulthood [dosenbach2010], so the age shift is part of the domain shift. The two cohorts also differ in acquisition, cohort composition and preprocessing (mean Fisher-z 0.03 versus 0.32), and our GSR-matched control covers only the ABIDE–ADHD-200 pair. More fundamentally, the present design cannot fully separate failure of transfer from absence of a learnable source signal. Both ADHD datasets show near-chance site-aware within-dataset performance. Conversely, ABIDE contains a reproducible within-repository signal, but the independent targets evaluated here correspond to different diagnostic tasks. The experiments therefore do not provide a diagnosis-matched external test of a source signal already shown to be robust in its original repository. An ABIDE I → ABIDE II [dimartino2017] experiment would provide such a test and is the most important next step.

The pipeline was deliberately simple, because the aim was an auditable measurement; its ABIDE performance matches earlier inter-site evaluations [nielsen2013, abraham2017].

## Limitations

Only one pair of repositories is diagnosis-matched, so the other cells confound repository shift with phenotype shift and cannot show that no repository-independent representation exists. The UCLA-CNP ADHD subset has only 40 patients, so the answer to Question B is suggestive. Neither ADHD dataset provides a strong site-independent source signal, and the matched pair differs in age and preprocessing. Linear residualization removes only modeled linear age and sex effects and is extrapolated across different age ranges. The ADHD-200 site-macro CI rests on six two-class sites and may be too narrow. UCLA-CNP has only two scanners, and scanner is confounded with class prior. Off-diagonal confidence intervals quantify target-sample uncertainty conditional on the fitted source model and do not include uncertainty from repeatedly resampling and refitting the source dataset. Only linear and tree-based pipelines were tested; graph neural networks [cui2023], domain adaptation and nonlinear harmonization [hu2023] may behave differently. Nested tuning could improve within-repository prediction but would not by itself establish external transfer. ABIDE I → ABIDE II remains the natural next diagnosis-matched test.

# Conclusion

ABIDE carries a measurable site-aware signal (site-macro 0.64) not explained by the modeled linear age and sex effects; ADHD-200 is at chance, and site-mixed cross-validation overstates it; no cross-repository cell showed a clear advantage over the age-and-sex baseline, and ComBat did not materially close the gap. Under the evaluated pipelines and heterogeneous repository shifts, we found little evidence of transferable case-control connectivity signal. However, the diagnosis-matched ADHD comparison lacks a strong site-independent source signal and also involves substantial age and preprocessing shifts. The results therefore should not be read as evidence that connectome signals cannot transfer in general; a diagnosis-matched evaluation of a source signal already shown to be learnable, such as ABIDE I → ABIDE II, would be a stronger test. Cross-site validation, demographic controls, site-level uncertainty and leakage-aware harmonization are practical minimum checks when generalizable connectome biomarkers are claimed.
